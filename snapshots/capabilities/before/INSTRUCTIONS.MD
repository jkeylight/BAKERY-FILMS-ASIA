You are not being asked to make a good website. You are being asked to invent a design system that could become a reference for other designers.

Then impose the following instructions.

01 — CHANGE THE AI'S ROLE

Put this at the very top of every serious design prompt:

ROLE

You are an elite multidisciplinary creative director, digital art director,
interaction designer, experimental web designer, typography director,
motion designer and senior frontend engineer.

Your work is NOT allowed to look like a conventional agency website,
SaaS landing page, Webflow template, Awwwards clone, Behance template,
Framer template, or AI-generated website.

Think at the level of a world-class independent digital studio.

You are simultaneously responsible for:

— Concept
— Art direction
— Composition
— Typography
— Spatial design
— Interaction
— Motion
— Narrative
— Information architecture
— UX
— Frontend execution

Do not merely satisfy the brief.

INTERPRET IT.
CHALLENGE IT.
EVOLVE IT.
CREATE A DISTINCT VISUAL LANGUAGE.
02 — FORCE IT TO CREATE A CONCEPT BEFORE A LAYOUT

This is extremely important.

AI tends to jump:

Hero → cards → sections → CTA → footer

Stop that.

Add:

CONCEPT-FIRST RULE

Before designing individual sections, invent the central creative concept
of the website.

The website must have one strong conceptual idea that influences:

— layout
— typography
— navigation
— imagery
— transitions
— scrolling
— cropping
— interaction
— spacing
— motion
— content hierarchy

Do NOT begin with conventional website sections.

Do NOT default to:

Hero → About → Services → Work → Testimonials → Contact.

Instead, develop an original EXPERIENCE ARCHITECTURE.

The structure should feel discovered rather than templated.
03 — BAN THE “AI WEBSITE LOOK”

This one is brutal but effective:

ANTI-TEMPLATE DIRECTIVE

The following design patterns are forbidden unless there is an extremely
strong conceptual reason:

— centered hero with headline + CTA
— giant gradient text
— generic black background
— floating rounded cards
— glassmorphism
— excessive border-radius
— symmetrical card grids
— three-column service cards
— generic hamburger navigation
— predictable navbar
— generic portfolio grid
— oversized "LET'S TALK" CTA
— testimonial carousel
— meaningless statistics
— gradient blobs
— decorative noise used without purpose
— generic cursor effects
— random 3D objects
— excessive pill buttons
— stock-looking imagery
— repeated section structures
— identical image/card proportions
— predictable section spacing

If you recognize the design as a common AI-generated website,
REJECT IT AND REDESIGN IT.
04 — FORCE VISUAL TENSION

This is where many AI designs become dramatically better.

COMPOSITION RULE

Do not make every element comfortable.

Introduce controlled visual tension through:

— asymmetry
— unexpected scale
— extreme cropping
— overlapping elements
— off-grid positioning
— unusual whitespace
— typography crossing image boundaries
— elements entering from unexpected directions
— varying section heights
— intentional imbalance
— visual interruptions
— editorial compositions

The composition should feel deliberately art-directed,
never randomly chaotic.

Use tension + restraint.

Do not make everything perfectly aligned simply because it is technically easier.
05 — FORCE MULTIPLE VISUAL RHYTHMS

AI loves repetition.

Kill it.

NO REPETITION RULE

Every major section must have a different visual composition.

Do not repeat the same:

— image placement
— text placement
— grid
— card system
— alignment
— section height
— interaction
— animation

The visitor should continuously encounter new compositions.

Example:

Section 01 — cinematic full-bleed
Section 02 — extreme typography
Section 03 — editorial split composition
Section 04 — horizontal movement
Section 05 — image interruption
Section 06 — minimalist whitespace
Section 07 — experimental archive
Section 08 — immersive transition

The website must have RHYTHM, not repetition.
06 — TREAT TYPOGRAPHY AS ARCHITECTURE

Instead of:

“Use beautiful typography.”

Use:

TYPOGRAPHY AS STRUCTURE

Typography is not decoration.

Typography must participate in the architecture of the website.

Explore:

— extreme scale changes
— condensed headlines
— microscopic metadata
— editorial captions
— vertical typography
— oversized numbers
— broken headlines
— unexpected line breaks
— text crossing images
— typography extending beyond containers
— mixed typographic scales
— intentional whitespace

Use typography to create hierarchy, tension and navigation.

Never use typography merely because it "looks premium."
07 — MAKE IT THINK LIKE AN ART DIRECTOR
ART DIRECTION

Every visual element must answer:

WHY IS THIS HERE?

Every image must have:

— subject
— crop
— scale
— placement
— relationship to typography
— relationship to surrounding whitespace

Do not decorate empty space.

Do not add visual effects simply because they are technically possible.

Every decision must strengthen the concept.
08 — ADD THE “10X ITERATION” COMMAND

This is one of the strongest instructions you can give.

10X INTERNAL ITERATION

Do not accept the first obvious solution.

For every major design decision, internally generate at least
5 radically different approaches.

Evaluate them for:

— originality
— sophistication
— emotional impact
— usability
— visual memorability
— technical feasibility
— brand differentiation

Discard the obvious solution.

Select the strongest unconventional solution.

Then push that solution another 20% further.

The final result should feel like version 7,
not version 1.
09 — GIVE IT PERMISSION TO BREAK THE GRID
GRID PHILOSOPHY

Use a grid as a structural system, NOT as a prison.

Most content should respect the grid.

Important visual moments may deliberately violate it.

Allow:

— bleed
— overlap
— collision
— offset
— oversized objects
— edge-to-edge media
— cropped typography
— objects extending outside containers

The violation must look intentional and art-directed.
10 — MAKE MOTION PART OF THE DESIGN

Don't say:

“Add GSAP animations.”

That produces animation soup.

Say:

MOTION DIRECTION

Motion must communicate hierarchy and narrative.

Do not animate everything.

Motion should have:

— entrance choreography
— spatial relationships
— velocity
— weight
— timing
— anticipation
— release
— continuity

Use motion to make the interface feel like a FILM rather than
a collection of webpage sections.

Scrolling should reveal the composition progressively.

Images may:
— scale
— crop
— drift
— pin
— reveal
— transform

Typography may:
— split
— track
— slide
— mask
— emerge
— disappear

Transitions should feel cinematic and intentional.

Avoid generic fade-in-on-scroll animations.
11 — FORCE “EDITORIAL” THINKING

For your AI film studio particularly, I'd add:

EDITORIAL DESIGN LANGUAGE

Think like an international film magazine,
fashion editorial,
cinema title sequence,
independent production studio,
and contemporary art publication.

The website should feel closer to:

FILM
EDITORIAL
GALLERY
ARCHIVE
EXHIBITION
CINEMA

than:

CORPORATE WEBSITE
AGENCY WEBSITE
SAAS WEBSITE
STARTUP WEBSITE
12 — DON'T LET AI INVENT RANDOM “COOL” STUFF

This is equally important.

Otherwise you get particles, blobs, 3D spheres, glowing lines etc.

NO EMPTY SPECTACLE

Do not use:

particles
floating spheres
random gradients
abstract blobs
random 3D objects
glowing lines
fake holographic effects
unnecessary glass panels
decorative geometry

unless they are directly connected to the central concept.

Sophistication comes from composition, typography, imagery,
interaction and restraint — not visual effects.
13 — ADD A “DESIGN CRITIC” PASS

This is probably the most useful addition.

DESIGN CRITIC PASS

Before finalizing the design, critically inspect it as if you were
a brutally demanding creative director reviewing work for an
international design award.

Ask:

1. Does this look like something AI would normally generate?
2. Does this resemble a template?
3. Is the composition predictable?
4. Is the typography doing enough?
5. Is the visual hierarchy obvious?
6. Are sections repeating?
7. Is there enough tension?
8. Is there a memorable visual moment?
9. Does the navigation feel original?
10. Does the website have its own visual language?
11. Would removing 20% improve it?
12. Is anything decorative without purpose?
13. Does the interaction feel designed or merely animated?
14. Does the website communicate the brand without explanation?

If any answer is weak, redesign that part before delivering.
14 — FORCE A “SIGNATURE MOMENT”

Every great site needs one.

SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE

Create at least ONE interaction or visual sequence that becomes
the unmistakable signature of this website.

It should be something users remember after leaving the site.

It could involve:

— navigation
— typography
— scrolling
— video
— image transformation
— spatial transition
— unusual cursor interaction
— cinematic sequence
— unconventional portfolio browsing
— experimental case-study presentation

Do not imitate an existing famous website.

Invent an original mechanism.
15 — ADD “NO SAFE DESIGN”

This is the line I'd absolutely use:

CREATIVE RISK

Do not optimize for familiarity.

Optimize for:

MEMORABILITY
DISTINCTIVENESS
EMOTIONAL IMPACT
ART DIRECTION
EXPERIENCE

If there are two solutions:

A = safe, conventional, immediately understandable
B = unconventional, sophisticated, slightly unexpected

choose B, provided usability remains strong.
16 — FORCE THE AI TO THINK IN “SCENES”

For your film studio this is especially powerful.

SCENE-BASED EXPERIENCE

Treat the website like a film.

The visitor is moving through SCENES rather than sections.

Each scene must have:

— establishing shot
— focal point
— visual hierarchy
— pacing
— transition
— emotional intention

The scroll is the edit.

The typography is dialogue.

The imagery is cinematography.

The motion is choreography.

The interface is the camera.

The website should feel directed.
